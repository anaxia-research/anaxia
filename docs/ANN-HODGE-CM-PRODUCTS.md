# Explicit CM divisor products and a sharp degree cutoff

We construct rational Hodge classes on products of complex multiplication elliptic curves from explicit divisors and check the construction over the rationals. Divisor generation for elliptic products is known. We also determine how large the effective divisor degree must be to see each isogeny direction on a product of two curves. [Imai's product theorem](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kodaimath1949/27/3/27_3_367/_article/-char/en) · [Rosen and Shnidman on the Néron–Severi lattice](https://arxiv.org/abs/1402.2233)

## Cycle construction

Choose a trace-zero endomorphism $\theta$ of a CM elliptic curve $E$ with $\theta^2=-d$ for a positive integer $d$. On $H^1(E,\mathbb Q)$ choose a nonzero $e$ and set $f=\theta^*e$. These are independent. Coordinate point fibers on $E^n$ give $e_j\wedge f_j$ up to common rational rescaling. Pull back a point by $z_j+z_k$ and $z_j+\theta z_k$ and subtract the diagonal terms. The two classes so obtained are, up to sign and rational rescaling,

$$
e_j\wedge f_k-f_j\wedge e_k,\qquad d e_j\wedge e_k+f_j\wedge f_k.
$$

Extend $T(e)=f$, $T(f)=-d e$ by derivation. In degree two the displayed classes together with the coordinate classes form a basis of the kernel. They have disjoint supports and the kernel has dimension $n^2$, from the two opposite eigenspaces on $H^1(E^n,\mathbb C)$. In degree $2p$, the kernel is the $(p,p)$ space. Over $\mathbb C$ each exterior basis vector in it factors into $p$ opposite-eigenvalue pairs. The divisor products therefore span over $\mathbb C$, and over $\mathbb Q$ by rank.

For a mixed product, group curves by imaginary quadratic field. Curves in the same block are isogenous by commensurability after scaling their period lattices. Pullback along an isogeny and its rational inverse given by pushforward divided by degree preserve algebraic cohomology classes. Thus work with a power of one curve per block.

Align the signs of the $\theta_j$ on their $(1,0)$ lines. The $(p,p)$ space is the degree-$2p$ kernel of $\sum_j T_j/\sqrt{d_j}$, where $T_j$ acts only in its own block. The coefficients $1/\sqrt{d_j}$ are rationally independent because their distinct square classes correspond to different character eigenspaces in the real multiquadratic field. Hence a rational class in the kernel is killed by every $T_j$. In each Künneth multidegree the simultaneous kernel is the tensor product of the block kernels, and it is generated by divisor products. If the multiplicities are $n_j$, the rank in codimension $p$ is the coefficient of $t^p$ in $\prod_j\sum_q\binom{n_j}{q}^2t^q$.

## Divisor degree on a product of two elliptic curves

For $A=E\times F$ with the product principal polarization $L$, the integral Néron–Severi lattice has two diagonal directions and off-diagonal lattice $\operatorname{Hom}(F,E)$. The Rosati matrix of a class is

$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}a&\phi\\\phi^\dagger&b\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The degree $L\cdot D$ is $a+b$. The diagonal directions come from coordinate point fibers. To obtain the specified off-diagonal, pull back a point by $(z,w)\mapsto z+\phi(w)$ and adjust the two diagonal terms. The diagonal entries of an effective class are nonnegative, and the matrix is positive semidefinite since an effective divisor on an abelian variety is nef by translation. In Hermitian coordinates normalized by the two principal polarizations, $|\phi|^2=\deg\phi$. Thus $ab\ge\deg\phi$.

For an integer $B\ge1$, any degree-$\le B$ effective class has $\deg\phi\le\lfloor B^2/4\rfloor$. Conversely choose $a=\lfloor B/2\rfloor$ and $b=B-a$ for any homomorphism under that bound. The resulting integral class is positive semidefinite. For a singular form, take the quotient by the connected radical, which is a complex subtorus because the form is rational on the lattice. The alternating Riemann form descends integrally to the quotient lattice and becomes positive definite. An ample line bundle with that form has a section by Riemann–Roch and vanishing. Its pullback gives the desired effective class on $A$. The dimension of the rational span of effective classes of degree at most $B$ is therefore two plus the rank of the span of these bounded-degree homomorphisms.

For a CM self-product of order discriminant $\Delta<0$, every nonreal order element has imaginary part at least $\sqrt{|\Delta|}/2$ in absolute value. This gives the lower bound $B\ge\lceil\sqrt{|\Delta|}\rceil$ to reach the fourth rational divisor direction. The usual order generator $(\epsilon+\sqrt\Delta)/2$, with $\epsilon=0$ or $1$ according to the parity of $\Delta$, has norm $(|\Delta|+\epsilon)/4$. At $B=\lceil\sqrt{|\Delta|}\rceil$ we have $\lfloor B^2/4\rfloor\ge (|\Delta|+\epsilon)/4$, using that a square is never $3$ modulo $4$. This attains the threshold. The extra CM directions can therefore require arbitrarily large degrees. A spreading proof for a fixed flat class still needs a characterization of which directions persist.

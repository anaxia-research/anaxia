# Explicit Hodge cycles on powers of the elliptic curve with period i

Let $E=\mathbb C/(\mathbb Z+i\mathbb Z)$. On $E^n$, the rational Hodge classes of every codimension are generated by products of explicitly given divisors. The following argument gives the divisors and an exact rank check in powers one through four. It works on the specified complex multiplication family.

Write $e_j=dx_j$ and $f_j=dy_j$ for the integral degree-one cohomology generators from the $j$th coordinate $x_j+iy_j$. The cohomology algebra over $\mathbb Q$ is the exterior algebra on these $2n$ generators. Let $T$ denote the derivation induced by $T e_j=f_j$ and $T f_j=-e_j$. Over $\mathbb C$, its two degree-one eigenspaces are the $(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ spaces, with eigenvalues $-i$ and $i$. In degree $2p$ the kernel is the $(p,p)$ space and has dimension $\binom np^2$. Since the derivation is rational, its rational kernel has the same dimension over $\mathbb Q$.

For each coordinate, pull a point back along the projection $z\mapsto z_j$. Its class is $e_j\wedge f_j$. For each pair $j<k$, pull a point back along $z\mapsto z_j+z_k$ and $z\mapsto z_j+iz_k$. These are algebraic morphisms of abelian varieties since multiplication by $i$ is an endomorphism of $E$. After subtracting the two coordinate classes, the pullbacks give

$$
e_j\wedge f_k+e_k\wedge f_j,\qquad e_j\wedge e_k+f_j\wedge f_k.
$$

These $n^2$ classes are independent by their disjoint supports in the exterior basis. They lie in the degree-two kernel and therefore form a basis. After complexification the degree-two kernel is spanned by $u\wedge v$, where $u$ has type $(1,0)$ and $v$ has type $(0,1)$. Every exterior basis vector of type $(p,p)$ can be reordered, up to sign, into a product of $p$ such pairs. Hence the divisor products span after complexification. The multiplication map is rational, so its image already has full rank over $\mathbb Q$. Products of divisor classes are algebraic by compatibility of the cycle class map with intersection products.

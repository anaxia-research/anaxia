# Explicit cycles and correspondences for F2 and F3′

In *Dense Algebraic Loci of Hodge Classes*, F2 is the rational Hodge conjecture on all complex abelian varieties. F3′ asks that every rational Hodge class on a smooth projective variety be a sum of images of Hodge classes on abelian varieties under algebraic correspondences. The source variety may be a point. Together the two statements imply the full rational Hodge conjecture, since algebraic correspondences take algebraic cycle classes to algebraic cycle classes. The constructions below cover specified families. [Definitions in the source manuscript](https://www.preprints.org/frontend/manuscript/6658a032f948caa6f4979515ce10c0a5/download_pub)

## F2 on products of elliptic curves

Work up to isogeny and group isogenous factors. The Hodge group of an elliptic product is the product of the block groups, each a norm-one torus for a CM curve or \(\mathrm{SL}_2\) for a non-CM curve. Thus it suffices to construct the invariant classes in the exterior algebra of each block. [Imai's product theorem](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/kodaimath1949/27/3/27_3_367/_article/-char/en)

For \(E^n\) with \(E\) non-CM, put \(V=H^1(E,\mathbb Q)\). The invariant tensors in \(V^{\otimes 2p}\) are generated by products of determinant pairings. The same generators in exterior degree \(2p\) follow by applying the alternating projection to the tensor algebra of the \(n\) copies of \(V\). For degree two the pairings are represented by coordinate point fibers and by
\[
(z_i+z_j)^*[0]-z_i^*[0]-z_j^*[0].
\]
Their intersections give the higher-degree generators. In a CM block the explicit construction also uses \((z_i+\theta z_j)^*[0]\), where \(\theta\) is a trace-zero endomorphism. Products of block generators and pushforward along an isogeny give the stated classes on the original elliptic product. This supplies algebraic cycles in the elliptic case of F2. [Exact CM block replay and cycle classes](ANN-HODGE-CM-PRODUCTS.md)

The sixfold construction in the source illustrates a separate difficulty. Suppose \(X\) is a simple abelian fourfold with \(\operatorname{End}^0(X)=\mathbb Q\) and \(Z\) is an abelian sixfold with a unital action of a degree-six field. There can be no nonzero homomorphism \(X\to Z\). Poincaré reducibility would split off one \(X\)-factor up to isogeny. The remaining two-dimensional factor has no homomorphisms with \(X\), so projection of the endomorphism algebra onto the \(X\)-factor would embed the degree-six field into \(\mathbb Q\). Consequently divisor products in Künneth multidegree \((1,1,2)\) on \(X^2\times Z\) lie in the \(X\)–\(X\) polarization line tensored with divisor classes on \(Z\). A class in the nontrivial \(T_X\otimes T_Z\) summand of that multidegree requires a different cycle construction.

## F3′ on split hypersurfaces

Let \(H\) be a hypersurface whose equation is a sum of degree-\(d\) forms on disjoint blocks of one or two variables, with \(d\ge2\). Unary forms are nonzero monomials, and binary forms are squarefree. All hypersurfaces in the recursion below are smooth, because each binary gradient can vanish only at the zero vector.

For at least four variables, divide the blocks into two groups with at least two variables in each, writing the equation as \(F(x)+G(y)=0\). Consider \(S_F:F(x)=u^d\), \(S_G:G(y)=v^d\), and the map
\[
([x:u],[y:v])\longmapsto [vx:\lambda u y],\qquad \lambda^d=-1 .
\]
The base ideal is \((u,v)\) because neither \(x\) nor \(y\) can vanish as an entire tuple on the source. Let \(\pi:\widetilde Y\to S_F\times S_G\) be the blow-up along \(C=V(F)\times V(G)\), with exceptional divisor \(j:E\hookrightarrow\widetilde Y\) and projection \(p:E\to C\). The displayed map resolves to a morphism \(f:\widetilde Y\to H\). At a generic target \([X:Y]\), normalize \(x=X,y=Y\) and solve \(u^d=F(X)\), \(v=\lambda u\). The generic degree is \(d\). [Shioda and Katsura's original split construction](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/tmj1949/31/1/31_1_97/_pdf)

For a rational Hodge class \(\alpha\) on \(H\), decompose \(f^*\alpha\) by the codimension-two blow-up formula. With
\[
\beta=\pi_* f^*\alpha,\qquad \gamma=-p_*j^*f^*\alpha
\]
the projection formula gives
\[
d\alpha=f_*\pi^*\beta+f_*j_*p^*\gamma.
\]
The signs follow from \(j^*j_*=-c_1(\mathcal O_E(1))\) on classes pulled back from \(C\). All maps are induced by algebraic graphs. Each hypersurface in \(S_F,S_G,C\) has fewer variables than \(H\).

The recursion ends at points and curves. For a positive-genus curve, the transpose of its Abel–Jacobi graph gives a surjection from the cohomology of its Jacobian. The pullback is an isomorphism in degree one and sends an ample divisor class to a nonzero degree-two class. Degree zero is generated by the unit. A genus-zero curve has its cohomology generated by the unit and a point, so these correspondences may both start at a point. The resulting sums of correspondence maps are surjective on cohomology. Their products are surjective by Künneth. Rational Hodge lifts exist because polarizable rational Hodge structures split, using the polarizations to complement the kernels. Applying the displayed formula inductively proves F3′ on this family and its products.

## Actual cycles in degrees two through four

In degree two or three, the leaf curves have genus at most one. In degree four they have the form \(w^4=F(x,y)\) with \(F\) squarefree. The order-four automorphism \(w\mapsto iw\) acts on the three holomorphic one-forms with eigenvalues \(i,i,-1\). Indeed the adjunction residues have linear numerators \(x,y,w\) and the residue volume form gains the determinant \(i\). The sign eigenspace is the elliptic quotient under \(w\mapsto-w\). The complementary abelian surface carries \(\mathbb Q(i)\) with both holomorphic eigenlines at the same embedding. Its rational lattices split as two copies of the Hodge structure of a CM elliptic curve with period \(i\), so the surface is isogenous to the square of that curve. Thus every leaf Jacobian in these degrees is isogenous to an elliptic product.

### Direct construction from a specified Hodge class

For a product of such hypersurfaces in degrees two, three and four, use the following construction without assuming general F2 or F3′:

1. Compute the two cohomological inputs \(\beta,\gamma\) at each blow-up, and apply the corresponding rule to each factor of the product.
2. At the curve leaves, lift to elliptic products by the point and Abel–Jacobi correspondences, using the elliptic isogeny factors in degree four.
3. Expand the lifted Hodge class in the divisor products described above. In an exact cohomology basis, this is a rational linear system. Take the same rational combination of the cycle intersections.
4. Push the cycles through the two graph composites at each node and divide by \(d\). The displayed formula proves that the resulting cycle class equals the input.

The proof works for all rational Hodge classes on the stated products. For arbitrary smooth projective varieties, the abelian correspondence input can be missing. For instance, for the rank-nine transcendental structure with totally real cubic endomorphism field in the source, the polarization adjoint acts trivially on the field. An endomorphism given by a multiplier \(a\) is a rational similitude only if \(a^2\in\mathbb Q\), which forces \(a\in\mathbb Q\) in odd degree three. Those similitudes cannot supply the two nonrational multiplier directions.

For diagonal Fermat hypersurfaces in arbitrary degrees, the same recursion uses Fermat curves as leaves. The diagonal roots of unity act on the first cohomology of a Fermat curve with distinct characters indexed by pairs \((a,b)\) of nonzero residues with \(a+b\ne0\pmod d\). The holomorphic residue forms have \(1\le a,b<d\), \(a+b<d\), and the conjugates supply the remaining characters. The resulting commutative endomorphism algebra has rational dimension \(2g\), so the Jacobian is of CM type. Combining the explicit graph construction with the recent proof of the rational Hodge conjecture for CM abelian varieties gives the rational Hodge conjecture on products of diagonal Fermat hypersurfaces. This implication uses the cited CM theorem for the leaf classes. The previous degree-two-through-four recipe gives the leaf cycles directly by divisor products for the stated binary-block hypersurfaces. [CM result of van Hoften and Sempliner](https://hexagonmath.org/2610.00259v2)
